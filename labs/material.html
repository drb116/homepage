Actually, aluminum is much more thermally conductive than steel. Typical room-temperature values are roughly: Copper: about 400 W/m·K Aluminum: about 200–235 W/m·K Carbon steel: about 45–55 W/m·K Stainless steel: about 15 W/m·K So the lab’s order—copper first, aluminum second, steel third—is correct. What may feel misleading is that steel is often associated with pans, tools, and other objects that get hot. But a steel object can still become very hot while transferring heat along its length more slowly than aluminum.
